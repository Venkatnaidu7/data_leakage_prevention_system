# DLP Policy Engine Guide

## 1. Overview

The DLP Policy Engine evaluates findings generated by the detection pipeline against a set of prioritized, versioned rules. Based on matched criteria, the engine determines the appropriate security action and severity.

## 2. Policy Schema

Each policy is defined by the following attributes:

- **ID & Name:** Unique identifier and human-readable descriptive title.
- **Description:** Context on why this policy exists (e.g., regulatory compliance, secret hygiene).
- **Enabled (`bool`):** Whether the policy is active during live scans.
- **Priority (`int`):** Evaluation order. Lower integers indicate higher priority (e.g., priority `1` takes precedence over priority `10`).
- **Target Detectors (`list[str]`):** List of detector types this policy monitors (e.g. `["credit_card", "secret_key"]` or `["*"]` for any).
- **Match Threshold (`int`):** Minimum count of findings required to trigger the policy (default: `1`).
- **Action:**
  - `LOG`: Quietly record the finding in the database and audit trail without raising an operational alert.
  - `WARN`: Flag the event as an advisory warning.
  - `BLOCK`: Signal that the transaction/file transfer must be halted at integrated enforcement points.
  - `QUARANTINE`: Move the payload or reference into restricted isolation for compliance review.
  - `ESCALATE`: Automatically create a high-priority incident and notify security analysts.
- **Severity:** `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `CRITICAL`.
- **Dry-Run / Simulation Mode (`bool`):** When enabled on a policy or requested via API, the engine simulates what action *would* have been taken without mutating incident state.

## 3. Conflict Resolution Order

When multiple policies match findings within a single scan request, conflicts are resolved deterministically:

1. **Active Status:** Disabled policies are skipped.
2. **Priority Rank:** Lower numerical priority value wins (Priority `1` > Priority `2`).
3. **Severity Weight:** If priorities are identical, the higher severity action wins:
   `CRITICAL` > `HIGH` > `MEDIUM` > `LOW`.
4. **Action Weight:** If severity is also identical, the most restrictive action takes precedence:
   `BLOCK` > `QUARANTINE` > `ESCALATE` > `WARN` > `LOG`.

## 4. Policy Simulation Mode

Simulation mode allows security analysts and policy authors to test new rule sets against historical or synthetic test payloads. The engine produces an evaluation report containing:
- Matched detector findings.
- Policies triggered.
- Selected winning policy.
- Action rationale and explanation.
- Indication that no live incidents or blocks were triggered.

## 5. Built-in Baseline Policies

1. **PCI-DSS Cardholder Data Protection:**
   - Detectors: `credit_card`
   - Threshold: 1
   - Action: `BLOCK`
   - Severity: `CRITICAL`
2. **High-Entropy Secrets & API Keys:**
   - Detectors: `secret_key`
   - Threshold: 1
   - Action: `BLOCK`
   - Severity: `CRITICAL`
3. **Bulk PII Leakage Alert:**
   - Detectors: `email`, `phone`
   - Threshold: 5
   - Action: `ESCALATE`
   - Severity: `HIGH`
4. **General PII Monitoring:**
   - Detectors: `email`, `phone`
   - Threshold: 1
   - Action: `LOG`
   - Severity: `LOW`

## 6. Data Classification Engine

The system implements a four-tier classification model resolving detector findings to the highest applicable sensitivity tier:

| Tier | Sensitivity | Trigger Criteria & Detectors | Example Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`PUBLIC`** | Lowest (Rank 1) | No sensitive findings detected. | Public documentation, open marketing materials. |
| **`INTERNAL`** | Low (Rank 2) | Isolated business identifiers or single email addresses. | `analyst@company.com`, internal report headers. |
| **`CONFIDENTIAL`** | Medium (Rank 3) | PII, personal phone numbers, National IDs (US SSN), or bulk emails (>= 3). | `***-**-3456`, `***-***-0199`, customer lists. |
| **`RESTRICTED`** | Highest (Rank 4) | High-consequence secrets, payment card numbers (PCI), private keys, auth tokens. | `4532-****-****-5671`, `AKIAIOSFODNN7EXAMPLE`. |

### Deterministic Resolution
When multiple detectors trigger on a single document or stream, findings deterministically resolve to the highest applicable tier:
$$\text{Document Classification} = \max_{f \in \text{findings}} \text{Rank}(f)$$

## 7. Deterministic Risk Scoring Model

The risk engine computes a transparent, explainable 0–100 numerical score categorized into four risk bands:
- **`LOW`**: 0.0 – 24.9
- **`MEDIUM`**: 25.0 – 49.9
- **`HIGH`**: 50.0 – 74.9
- **`CRITICAL`**: 75.0 – 100.0

### Scoring Formula & Contributing Factors
The final score is computed as an additive model clamped between 0 and 100:

1. **Classification Baseline:**
   - `RESTRICTED`: +50 points
   - `CONFIDENTIAL`: +35 points
   - `INTERNAL`: +15 points
   - `PUBLIC`: +0 points
2. **Channel & Egress Modifier:**
   - `removable_media` (USB drive): +25 points
   - `clipboard`: +15 points
   - `file` (internal disk): +5 points
3. **Findings Volume & Confidence:**
   - Up to +20 points: $\min(\text{count} \times 4.0, 20.0) \times \text{Average Confidence}$
4. **Policy Severity Amplifier:**
   - `CRITICAL`: +15 points
   - `HIGH`: +10 points
   - `MEDIUM`: +5 points

## 8. Enforcement Truthfulness & Action Semantics

In accordance with defensive security engineering principles, this prototype strictly distinguishes policy intent from operating-system enforcement:

- **`ALLOW`**: The policy permits the observed activity without restriction.
- **`ALERT`**: The policy flagged the activity and generated an incident for security analyst review. No disruption of user activity occurred.
- **`BLOCK`**: The policy determined that the activity should be prevented.
- **`NOT_ENFORCED`**: The policy recommended `BLOCK`, but because the agent operates in non-invasive user space without kernel drivers, the operation was permitted at the OS level while recording an auditable security finding.
